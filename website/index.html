Engineering Context & Background

Research Foundations & Experimental Evaluation

SafeKid AI couples operational residential monitoring with rigorous offline scientific evaluation. Below are the quantitative takeaways, architectural specifications, and empirical benchmarks grounding the pipeline.

Computer Vision Research & Pipeline Prototype

AI-Assisted Child Safety Monitoring for Gated Residential Communities

An open, reproducible engineering initiative investigating real-time computer vision, multi-object tracking, and region-of-interest geofencing to assist in detecting unauthorized child presence in hazardous residential zones.

⚠️
Operational Assistance Disclaimer: SafeKid AI is designed strictly as a computer-vision assistance tool to aid human monitoring operators. It does not replace adult supervision, physical perimeter fencing, lifeguards, or emergency response personnel. The models and pipelines presented here reflect controlled experimental evaluations on automotive dashcam and exploratory surveillance samples and have not yet been certified for autonomous life-safety deployment.

The Community Safety Problem

Current Limitations

In gated residential communities, children can unintentionally wander into high-risk areas including swimming pool perimeters, vehicular driveways, and automated boom barrier access gates.

  • ✕ Human Monitoring Fatigue: Conventional CCTV requires continuous human attention; security guards monitoring multi-camera walls experience visual fatigue within 20 minutes.
  • ✕ Delayed Incident Awareness: Existing security setups record passive footage for post-incident review rather than surfacing timely visual notifications.
  • ✕ Complex Environmental Occlusion: Outdoor residential zones feature moving vehicles, gates, and trees that obscure pedestrian silhouettes.

The SafeKid AI Approach

Implemented Pipeline

SafeKid AI explores a multi-stage software pipeline combining generic pedestrian detection, temporal tracking, spatial polygon intersection, and human-in-the-loop review.

  • ✓ Person Detection & Tracking: Leverages neural object detectors (YOLOv8n / D-FINE-N) and ByteTrack to track pedestrian movements over time.
  • ✓ Spatial ROI Geofencing: Uses foot-contact point-in-polygon math to monitor calibrated hazard zones (Pool deck, Driveway perimeter, Gate swings).
  • ✓ Demographic Tracklet Estimation: Aggregates crop classifications over temporal tracks, maintaining an explicit UNKNOWN state to avoid ungrounded assumptions.

Empirical Detector Evaluation

Model Comparison: YOLOv8n vs D-FINE-N

Direct experimental comparison across 2,010 evaluated JAAD dashcam frames. Both models were evaluated using identical operating confidence thresholds ($\text{conf} \ge 0.25$) and standard global COCO evaluation protocol.

YOLOv8n Engineering Baseline
Anchor-Free CNN Detector (Ultralytics, 3.16M params)

Selected as the provisional engineering baseline for prototype pipeline integration due to its superior precision, minimal false alarm footprint, and real-time edge CPU performance.

  • ✓ High Operational Precision (87.80%): Produces only 853 false alarms across 2,010 frames (0.42 FP/frame), preventing operator alert fatigue.
  • ✓ Real-Time Edge CPU Throughput: Averages 106.43 ms per frame (9.40 FPS) on an Intel Core i3 CPU (2.72x faster than D-FINE-N).
  • ✓ Tighter Boundary Localization: Scores 0.7408 sequence-macro Mean IoU, minimizing bounding box jitter for foot-contact estimation.
  • ✕ Lower Recall on Small Pedestrians: Detects only 3.27% of distant pedestrians (<32px height) and 46.64% of pedestrian instances overall.
D-FINE-N Research Candidate
DETR-style Real-Time Transformer (4.45M params)

Identified as a high-sensitivity research candidate demonstrating superior recall on distant and partially occluded pedestrians, currently blocked from deployment by background false alarms.

  • ✓ Superior Overall Recall (56.60%): Successfully captured +1,311 more pedestrian instances (+9.96% recall advantage, p = 0.0039).
  • ✓ High Small-Pedestrian Sensitivity: Captures 23.09% of pedestrians under 32px height (7.07x higher sensitivity than YOLOv8n).
  • ✕ Severe False Positive Rate: Emits 10,410 false alarms (5.18 FP/frame, 41.71% precision), triggering massive alert fatigue in unadapted form.
  • ✕ Heavy CPU Compute Burden: Averages 289.42 ms per frame (3.46 FPS), requiring aggressive keyframe skipping to run on edge hardware.

Technical Root Cause Analysis

Architectural Trade-Off

Why does YOLOv8n miss 96.7% of small pedestrians while D-FINE-N captures nearly a quarter?

  • ✓ Fine-grained Distribution Focal Loss (D-FINE): D-FINE-N's weighting decoders maintain spatial coordinate probability distributions across sub-pixel bins, preserving small object signals that standard anchor-free CNN downsampling attenuates.
  • ✕ CNN Stride Limitations (YOLOv8): With $P_3/P_4/P_5$ feature pyramids (downsampling strides of 8, 16, 32), an object under 24 pixels projects to fewer than $3 \times 3$ feature cells, leading to feature collapse.
  • ✕ Trade-Off with Background Sensitivity: D-FINE-N's high sensitivity to small structures directly causes its high false alarm rate (10,410 FPs), as background foliage and textures trigger query decoders.

Scientific Methodology

Datasets & Evaluation Methodology

Experimental benchmarks were conducted across two public pedestrian video datasets, each representing distinct viewing angles, frame rates, and lighting conditions.

JAAD (Primary Pedestrian Benchmark)

2,010 Evaluated Frames
  • ✓ Dataset Identity: Joint Attention in Autonomous Driving (JAAD) benchmark dataset.
  • ✓ Evaluated Volume: 9 sequences, 2,010 evaluated frames, 13,161 ground-truth annotations.
  • ✓ Demographic Diversity: 15 active child tracks (1,936 boxes), 31 adult tracks (4,735 boxes), and 6,490 unclassified pedestrian boxes.
  • ✕ Domain Limitation: Forward-facing automotive dashcam video recorded at street level; does not represent elevated residential CCTV angles.

VIRAT (Supplementary Surveillance Sample)

67 Evaluated Frames
  • ✓ Dataset Identity: Video Image Retrieval and Analysis Tool (VIRAT) surveillance fixture.
  • ✓ Evaluated Volume: 67 frames, 134 ground-truth annotations across 2 adult pedestrian tracks.
  • ✓ Viewing Perspective: Elevated CCTV camera mounted at ~30° downward angle overlooking parking area.
  • ✕ Domain Limitation: Very small supplementary exploratory sample (0 child pedestrians); not sufficient for broad residential CCTV validation.

Evaluation Metric Definitions & Interpretations

Reference Standards
Metric Mathematical Formula Operational Interpretation in Residential Child Safety
True Positive (TP) $\text{Predicted box matching GT with IoU} \ge 0.50$ A person is correctly detected in the camera frame.
False Positive (FP) $\text{Predicted box with no matching GT}$ False alarm (phantom box). Drives operator alert fatigue.
False Negative (FN) $\text{Ground-truth person missed by detector}$ Missed detection. In a hazardous zone, a child is undetected.
Precision $\frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FP}}$ Fraction of alarms that correspond to real people. (YOLO: 87.80%, D-FINE: 41.71%).
Recall $\frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FN}}$ Fraction of all actual people detected. (YOLO: 46.64%, D-FINE: 56.60%).
F1-Score $2 \cdot \frac{\text{Precision} \cdot \text{Recall}}{\text{Precision} + \text{Recall}}$ Harmonic mean balancing false alarms against missed detections.
AP@0.50 $\int_0^1 P(r) \, dr \text{ at IoU} \ge 0.50$ Area under precision-recall curve across all confidence ranks.
Intersection over Union $\frac{\text{Area}(B_p \cap B_g)}{\text{Area}(B_p \cup B_g)}$ Box overlap tightness; critical for foot-contact ground projection.

Governance & Compliance

Ethics, Privacy & Deployment Limitations

Responsible AI principles, privacy safeguards, and architectural constraints governing SafeKid AI.

Privacy by Design Safeguards

Core Principles
  • ✓ No Facial Recognition: SafeKid AI does not incorporate facial recognition, biometric face embedding, or identity matching. Silhouettes and pedestrian crops are processed purely for bounding-box tracking and coarse age-bracket estimation.
  • ✓ No Exact Age Prediction: The system classifies crops into broad categories (CHILD, ADULT, UNKNOWN) and does not attempt continuous age regression (e.g. "7 years old").
  • ✓ Mandatory UNKNOWN State: Whenever low resolution, heavy occlusion, or ambiguous geometry exists, the classifier defaults to UNKNOWN rather than guessing.
  • ✓ Human-in-the-Loop Verification: Consequential safety alerts require human security confirmation before triggering physical alarms or community notifications.

Deployment Prerequisites & Limitations

Pre-Deployment Requirements
  • ✕ Not Validated in Live Residential CCTV: Evaluated on automotive dashcam and 67-frame surveillance fixtures. A live residential community pilot has not yet been executed.
  • ✕ Community Consent Required: Prior to any physical pilot installation, explicit residential community authorization, privacy disclosures, and tenant consent must be secured.
  • ✕ Strict Data Retention Policies: Video streams must be processed ephemerally in edge device memory, retaining only anonymized incident event logs.
  • ✕ No Life-Safety Certification: SafeKid AI is not certified as a medical device, life-safety system, or autonomous emergency dispatch system.
